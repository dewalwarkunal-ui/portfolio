Origin

I started as an architect, where I learned that good outcomes depend as much on execution as on design. Approach

How I solve problems

I believe effective problem solving begins with understanding, not assumptions. Before recommending changes, I take the time to understand the current state by engaging with stakeholders, analysing processes, and identifying where challenges originate. Rather than addressing issues in isolation, I examine how operations, processes, communication, accountability, financial management, and data interact within the broader system. This enables me to identify root causes and implement solutions that deliver meaningful, sustainable improvements.

Legacy Platform to Zoho: A Structured Transformation

The Mission — Why we had to act

Our project management system was no longer serving the evolving needs of our teams. It slowed us down, frustrated users, and lacked the adaptability modern teams demand. The mission: design and deliver a tool that helps teams collaborate smarter, work faster, and lead with clarity.

"I'm not always clear on what to work on or prioritise." — Design Team, User Sentiment Survey

Feedback like this, alongside comments such as "the dashboard gives no clear visibility" and "we end up avoiding the old platform for real collaboration," made the case for change concrete rather than theoretical.

The Problem — Six interconnected gaps

Before proposing any changes, I ran structured interviews across the team. The friction wasn't isolated to one system complaint. It spanned six linked areas: org structure, financial management, operational efficiency, PM & accountability, communication, and data analysis.

Approach — A 40-week, evidence-led transformation

  • Weeks 1–3, Preparation: identified core challenges with the legacy platform, defined success criteria, aligned leadership on goals.
  • Weeks 4–9, Vendor Research: shortlisted Monday.com, Zoho, Microsoft Planner, and ClickUp; narrowed to Monday.com and Zoho after internal feedback and needs assessment.
  • Weeks 10–19, Pilot & Evaluation: a cross-functional pilot group, spanning Account Management, Project Management, Design, and Finance, ran hands-on demos and testing, scored on usability, reporting, automation, and scalability.
  • Weeks 20–40, Customisation & Go-Live: configured Zoho Projects, CRM, and Books to match real workflows, with role-based dashboards and automation.

Zoho was selected through a collaborative, KPI-driven evaluation with real user input. It wasn't a top-down decision.

Change Management — "Say Hello to Zoho"

A platform switch fails or succeeds on adoption, not configuration. Starting one week before launch, I ran a structured, week-by-week communication campaign:

  • Week 1, Awareness: "Zoho is coming!" — shared why the change was happening and what to expect.
  • Week 2, Education & Engagement: tool capabilities, early benefits, testimonials from the pilot group.
  • Week 3, Training & Support: role-specific training sessions, SharePoint hub with tutorials and guides.
  • Week 4, Launch Plan: confirmed launch date, plus a final checklist covering logins, sessions, and who to contact for help.

Training was tailored by function — Project Managers, Account Managers, Designers, and Finance each had customised sessions, supported by detailed user manuals, FAQs, and step-by-step video tutorials. During launch week, Zoho representatives were onsite for real-time support, and the Operations team tracked every reported blocker for fast escalation and resolution.

To manage the risk of migrating legacy data, I chose a controlled overlap strategy: ongoing and critical projects were closed out on the legacy platform, while new projects started fresh in Zoho. This minimised data loss without an all-at-once cutover.

Exhibit 02 · Atlas Copco

Standardising the Brand Studio's Way of Working

Alongside the Zoho migration, I standardised how work actually moves through the studio, from first lead to final invoice, with clear ownership at every stage.

Flow
Lead → Opportunity → Project → Invoicing
Roles defined

Background — One studio, dozens of informal variations

A platform migration alone doesn't fix inconsistent ways of working. It just moves the inconsistency onto new software. Alongside the Zoho transition, I designed and documented a single, unified process flow covering every stage of studio work, from the moment a lead enters the system to final invoicing, with explicit turnaround times and named ownership at every step.

The Framework

Stage 01 — Lead Handling
TAT: 4-hour first response. Verify service scope fit, capture brief details, structured follow-up cadence (Day 3 → Day 30). Led by Account Manager.
Stage 02 — Opportunity Handling
TAT: 24hrs standard / 48hrs complex. Scope definition & estimate creation, proposal review and approval (BL/GM), present proposal to customer. AM + Delivery Lead.
Stage 03 — Project Handling
TAT: based on scope. Resource & schedule planning, self-QC + Creative Manager QC before delivery, customer acceptance & "First Time Right" tracking. Led by Delivery Lead.
Stage 04 — Invoicing & Closure
TAT: 24hrs standard / 48hrs custom. Billing status check & invoice creation, discrepancy resolution, SAP reconciliation & archive. Led by Business Controller.

Why It Mattered — Governance you can actually audit

Every stage above carries an explicit turnaround-time commitment and a named accountable role: Account Manager, Delivery Lead, Creative Manager, Business Controller, Business Lead, or General Manager. This removes ambiguity in escalation paths and ownership. This was one of the 20+ SOPs built during the Atlas Copco engagement, and it's the operational backbone the Zoho platform was ultimately configured to run.
