Case Study 01 · Atlas Copco

Legacy Platform to Zoho: A Structured Transformation

37-Week Programme 70+ Users Global Brand Studio 2025

View Case Study →

The Mission

Why we had to act

Our project management system was no longer serving the evolving needs of our teams. It slowed us down, frustrated users, and lacked the adaptability modern teams demand. The mission: design and deliver a tool that helps teams collaborate smarter, work faster, and lead with clarity.

Daily time entry is the most frustrating part of my day.

Design Team, User Sentiment Survey

Feedback like this, alongside comments such as "the dashboard gives no clear visibility" and "we end up avoiding the old platform for real collaboration", made the case for change concrete rather than theoretical.

The Problem

Six interconnected gaps

Before proposing any changes, I ran structured interviews across the team. The friction wasn't isolated to one system complaint. It spanned six linked areas:

01Org Structure
02Financial Management
03Operational Efficiency
04PM & Accountability
05Communication
06Data Analysis
Approach

A 37-week, evidence-led transformation

  • Weeks 1–3, Preparation: Identified core challenges with the legacy platform, defined success criteria, aligned leadership on goals.
  • Weeks 4–9, Vendor Research: Shortlisted Monday.com, Zoho, Microsoft Planner, and ClickUp; narrowed to Monday.com and Zoho after internal feedback and needs assessment.
  • Weeks 10–19, Pilot & Evaluation: A cross-functional pilot group, made up of Account Management, Project Management, Design, and Finance, ran hands-on demos and testing, scored on usability, reporting, automation, and scalability.
  • Weeks 20–40, Customisation & Go-Live: 4–5 months configuring Zoho Projects, CRM, and Books to match real workflows, with role-based dashboards and automation.

Zoho was selected through a collaborative, KPI-driven evaluation with real user input. It wasn't a top-down decision.

Change Management

"Say Hello to Zoho": the internal campaign

A platform switch fails or succeeds on adoption, not configuration. Starting one week before launch, I ran a structured, week-by-week communication campaign:

  • Week 1, Awareness: "Zoho is coming!" This shared why the change was happening and what to expect.
  • Week 2, Education & Engagement: Tool capabilities, early benefits, testimonials from the pilot group.
  • Week 3, Training & Support: Role-specific training sessions, SharePoint hub with tutorials and guides.
  • Week 4, Launch Plan: Confirmed launch date, plus a final checklist covering logins, sessions, and who to contact for help.

Training was tailored by function. Project Managers, Account Managers, Designers, and Finance each had customised sessions, supported by detailed user manuals, FAQs, and step-by-step video tutorials. During launch week, Zoho representatives were onsite for real-time support, and the Operations team tracked every reported blocker for fast escalation and resolution.

To manage the risk of migrating legacy data, I chose a controlled overlap strategy: ongoing and critical projects were closed out on the legacy platform, while new projects started fresh in Zoho. This minimised data loss without an all-at-once cutover.

Outcome

Results

60%Cost Reduction
7.8/10Adoption Score
ZeroDowntime
10New Capability Gains

Annual system cost dropped from roughly $36.8K to $14.6K, a 60% saving for a platform with more modules rather than fewer. The migration ran with full operational continuity: zero downtime and zero data loss, plus a 7.8 out of 10 adoption satisfaction score, with user complaints reduced significantly compared to the legacy system. The transition also recorded the highest invoicing value the studio had seen, reflecting better-governed projects and a healthier delivery pipeline.

Case Study 02 · Atlas Copco

Standardising the Brand Studio's Way of Working

Lead → Opportunity → Project → Invoicing 9 Defined Roles

View Case Study →

Background

One studio, dozens of informal variations

A platform migration alone doesn't fix inconsistent ways of working. It just moves the inconsistency onto new software. Alongside the Zoho transition, I designed and documented a single, unified process flow covering every stage of studio work, from the moment a lead enters the system to final invoicing, with explicit turnaround times and named ownership at every step.

The Framework
Stage 01

Lead Handling

TAT: 4-hour first response
  • Verify service scope fit
  • Capture brief details
  • Structured follow-up cadence (Day 3 → Day 30)
Led by Account Manager
Stage 02

Opportunity Handling

TAT: 24hrs standard / 48hrs complex
  • Scope definition & estimate creation
  • Proposal review and approval (BL/GM)
  • Present proposal to customer
AM + Delivery Lead
Stage 03

Project Handling

TAT: based on scope
  • Resource & schedule planning
  • Self-QC + Creative Manager QC before delivery
  • Customer acceptance & "First Time Right" tracking
Led by Delivery Lead
Stage 04

Invoicing & Closure

TAT: 24hrs standard / 48hrs custom
  • Billing status check & invoice creation
  • Discrepancy resolution
  • SAP reconciliation & archive
Led by Business Controller
Why It Mattered

Governance you can actually audit

Every stage above carries an explicit turnaround-time commitment and a named accountable role: Account Manager, Delivery Lead, Creative Manager, Business Controller, Business Lead, or General Manager. This means escalation paths and ownership are never ambiguous. This was one of the 20+ SOPs built during the Atlas Copco engagement, and it's the operational backbone the Zoho platform was ultimately configured to run.
